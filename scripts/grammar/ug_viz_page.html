The grammar four LLMs agree on fits in three rules

Read on the same 1,000 sentences translated into 13 languages, Qwen2.5 (0.5B, 1.5B), Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma-2-2B hold one shared grammar in their mid-layer geometry. It is simpler than X-bar theory: the parts of X-bar the models do not use are simply left out.

The theory

Type

Every word gets a type from a small universal space of about 4–8 dimensions. The main axis runs from referents (names, numbers, nouns, adjectives) to relators (verbs, auxiliaries, adpositions, subordinators); the second from content words to function words.

cross-model category-geometry agreement 0.92–0.96 · rank-4 code keeps 95% of held-out-language accuracy · erasing it hurts prediction in 52/52 cells
Link

Every word hangs from exactly one head, with a typed link (subject, object, modifier…). Relators are heads: of → house, that → left. Determiners are not: house → the.

relator-head beats content-head in 47 of 52 model × language cells
Merge

A head takes its dependents one at a time, closest-bound first: complement, then modifiers, then subject. There are no bar levels, no specifier slots, and no empty projections. Word order is a separate per-language choice.

binary grouping +0.007 over flat links (37/52) · X-bar levels add nothing (14/52)

Evidence

Grouping fit is the rank correlation between a probe's word-to-word distances and a theory's tree distances, after removing word order and each word's height in the tree (which the word type alone predicts). The embedding layer scores about 0.07 and an untrained network 0.01, so the signal is learned context.

Tree

Empirical, not proved: linear probes, rank correlations and a LEACE erasure on four decoder LLMs (0.5–2.6B), one mid layer each plus depth checks, on UD PUD (news and Wikipedia). Theory trees are deterministic conversions of the gold UD trees. Tree edges themselves do not transfer across languages above an adjacent-word baseline; the shared structure is graded grouping.
