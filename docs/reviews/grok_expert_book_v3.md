# Review of the book v3 (Grok Expert)

Book-only review of `conlang/BUKE_DE_LORE_FIRA.md` after #214, pasted into the session by the user. The full review
is summarised below section by section, followed by notes from checking its claims against the book.

## Summary of the review

1. **Learning.** The arity endings, `pe`/`te`, `bi X pe Y`, `la`/`ka` and the pronoun leaves were learnable from the
   drawn trees and the contrast pairs. The reviewer got stuck on "undefined" particles (`si`, `pape`), on "citation
   form vs used form" (`pono`/`pona`, `sahenu`/`saheno`, `pu`/`tu` vs `pe`/`te`), on long clauses, on claim marks used
   before being taught, and on a dictionary ordered by Talema letters with no route in from page one.
2. **Translations.** The opening and teaching sentences were read with medium-high confidence; the leaf tale and the
   play with medium-low confidence, with glue words (`mide`, `nada`, `tila`, `kinada`, `sofata`, `sira`, `mosa`)
   marked as guessed because the reviewer could not find their roots.
3. **Problem sentences.** It claimed that `veloma` and `durema` do not end in *a*; that `deha` (ten) should be eight
   for `ia`; that `desi te bi … pe ma pe pesa` has two illegal subjects; that `vita` "far" will be read as "life"; that
   Sanko as a "small old program" is a wrong root.
4. **Teaching order.** The ideas come in a good order but the access path does not: page one is full Talema with no
   instruction to look roots up; the claim marks are used before they are taught; the dictionary is not a teaching
   index.
5. **Vocabulary.** Numbers should be one table. Rough words (*bitch*, *shit*, *dick*, *bullshit* …) sit unfenced
   beside core words. Some confusable sets (`ser`/`sev`/`sek`/`des`).
6. **Literature.** The leaf tale is the best literary idea; the play's first scene line is memorable; songs are too
   short; suggests one tree per tale and cutting the seed lecture.
7. **Fifteen concrete changes** (trees for page one, a numbers box, define `si`/`pape`/`mid`, a particle index, fence
   rough words, mark quotes with `kas`, move the claim marks before the tales, a closing tree for the leaf tale, a
   dictionary rule about citation endings).

## Notes (checked against the book)

- **One misreading explains most of the review.** Dictionary headwords follow the same arity rule as every word:
  `saheno veraba count-a zählen-a` is the root `sahen` with three children. The reviewer read `saheno`, `pono`,
  `su`, `pu` as citation forms. Hence the "undefined" `si` (it is `s` "and" with two children), the "missing" `mid`,
  `nad`, `til`, `kinad`, `sofat`, `sir`, `mos` (all present), the `pono`/`pona` and `pu`/`pe` "splits", and the
  "illegal" double `pe` (one belongs to the quoted clause, one to the verb of saying). **Fixed in v4** by writing
  headwords as bare roots with a hyphen (`sahen-o veraba …`), the mention form v3 introduced.
- **Wrong:** `ia` is 2×5 + 0 = 10, so `deha` (ten) is right; `veloma` and `durema` end in *a*; Sanko is meant to be a
  small old program.
- **Adopted in v4:** bare-root headwords; source words for the role particles; rough words fenced in their own section;
  a claim-mark section before the tales; a numbers table and the worked `ia`; a drawn quotation tree; a page-one
  pointer to the book of roots and a drawn first sentence; *future* instead of `vita` on page one; a closing tree for
  the leaf tale.
- **Not adopted:** marking every reported clause with `kas` (the quotation tree teaches the reading instead); cutting
  Amleto's seed passage (taste; another reviewer valued the scene); an English reverse index (the first-words chapter
  covers the core).
