# Review of PR #212 (Grok)

Pasted into the session by the user; kept as received. Where Grok's readings of Talema are wrong, the notes at the
end say so. Those misreadings were themselves useful evidence: the reviewer learned the grammar but guessed at
roots, which is why the v2 book teaches the core vocabulary early.

---

**Yes, you can learn to read Talema from the book alone**, but with friction and gaps. The three rules are taught clearly and early, the stack-machine reading procedure works, and the progressive introduction (counting → trees → small-words dialogue → tales) is well sequenced. A careful reader who already knows English/German/Spanish can bootstrap the closed class and the most frequent roots from context + the later dictionary. I did not get stuck on the pure grammar; I got stuck on **vocabulary acquisition and on a few underspecified conventions**.

### 1. Learning from the book alone — where it works and where it stalls

**What works**
- R3 (arity endings) is taught by pure counting and by the classic "Ana sees the dog / the new dog / the new dog in the wood" ladder. The drawn tree for `seru te bofe la nova ne vule la pe Ana-a` is exact and immediately usable.
- R2 (head-first) and R1 (relators) are demonstrated by contrast pairs and by the claim that every sentence ends in `a`.
- Relators head their complements (`si tova tova`, Polish-notation arithmetic; `lor` for mention; `tob` for yes/no questions) are introduced by example and then used consistently.
- Claim marks (`bove` / `sere` / `pefe`) and the "law of the marks" appear in the founding story and again in the agent-speech chapter.

**Where I got stuck**
- Early chapters use roots that have not yet been taught (`ledo`, `pesa`, `nora`, `seko`, `nage`, `vono`, `keni`, `hole`, `gepi`, `lovala`, `dalega` …).
- No systematic gloss or interlinear is ever given; the only help is the later root-book.
- Dependent order is declared free (emphasis), yet almost every example follows the same unmarked order.
- The book never states the full set of relators in one place.

### 2. Sample translations and problem sentences

- `ledo te buke tisa nova pe tada . ledu te pesa ne time vita mika pe tada .` ≈ "Read this new book, you. Read it in a little life-time, you."
- `seru te bofe la nova ne vule la pe Ana-a .` = "Ana sees the new dog in the wood."
- `sere vi te tipi mela ka pe lore keda .` ≈ "It is seen that there is a little type on every word."
- `pefe gogi vaha pe mide la .` ≈ "It is open whether the minds are one."
- The isolated `vaha .` "violates the 'every sentence ends in a' slogan."
- `si bo mela hera pe lore la bi biga pe gepe la .` "Mildly ambiguous for a first-time reader."
- `dori seka seke fa .` glossed as "do not speak the secret."

### 3. Language-design judgement (summary)

Strengths: three exceptionless rules; a pure stack machine; relators heading coordination; claim marks, mention and
`tob`; the waiting letters. Gaps: tense/aspect only as words; no plural strategy; content questions have no stated
rule; word formation is root coinage only; free order is never exercised; determiners postposed.

### 4. Vocabulary judgement (summary)

Closed class and top roots are well aligned. Flags: `pes`/`pesa`, `sel`/`sela` (mis-segmentation risk), `tar`
"they" close to `tad` "you", residual junk in the root book (`kapake pc-a`, `kakate aktie-a`). The coinages should be
listed early.

### 5. Concrete suggestions (as given)

1. A one-page small-words table after the three rules. 2. Rewrite `vaha .`. 3. Examples that vary dependent order.
4. A productive plural strategy. 5. State the content-question convention. 6. List the closed set of relators.
7. Move the coinages early. 8. A fully glossed debugging dialogue. 9. Filter remaining junk rows. 10. Note in
CONLANG.md that the book is normative for micro-conventions.

## Notes on this review (checked against the book)

- **Misreadings.** `seru te bofe la nova ne vule la pe Ana-a` is "Ana sees the sun now in the world" (`bof` sun,
  `nov` now, `vul` world), not "the new dog in the wood". The opening is "you read this book now; maybe you read it
  in a far time". `dori seka seke fa` is "to speak, or not to speak". `pefe gogi vaha pe mide la` is "open: why do
  the minds agree".
- **`vaha .` does end in *a*.** The objection is mistaken.
- **Rejected:** separating look-alikes such as `pes`/`pesa` (root plus ending, by design); changing `tar`/`tad`
  (roots are never reassigned); `kakate aktie-a` (German *Aktie*, a share, is correct).
- **Adopted in v2:** early core vocabulary with classes, the relator list, order varied for focus, the number/time
  conventions, the content-question convention, early coinages, a drawn tree in the dialogue, a dictionary pass,
  and the note that the book is normative.
